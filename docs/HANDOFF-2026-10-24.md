# HANDOFF: Assembly-to-Physics Verification in Lean 4

Snapshot 2026-10-24 · spec doc rev 46 · no Lean code written yet

Sections 1–6 brief you. Section 7 is the full spec, the contract for everything you build. Section 8 lists references. The living copy of the spec is the owner's doc at https://claude.ai/artifact/H3oYFUK9aPzhbBMcXqNm6w; you may not have access to it, so section 7 is a verbatim snapshot.

## 1. Mission

Build a Lean 4 + Mathlib library whose top theorem says: a chip built from a verified netlist and run inside its operating envelope executes a verified RV32I-subset program correctly, with probability at least 1 − (ε_init + ε_mfg + N·ε_cycle).

The physics is honest rather than first-principles:

- a few exact laws carry the weight: charge conservation, Faraday's law (via potentials) and the second law (as device passivity);
- devices are specified by measured inequalities, not formulas;
- a probabilistic shell charges every unmodelled effect to an explicit failure budget;
- every hypothesis is tagged with what justifies it.

## 2. Constraints from the project owner

1. **Do not use PhysLean or PhysLib** (`leanprover-community/physlib`, formerly HepLean and PhysLean), either as a dependency or as a source of definitions to copy. The owner and many physicists consider it unsuitable for this application. Build directly on Mathlib.
2. **No first-principles derivations of devices.** The owner wants an elegant library. The design they asked to have specified gets its elegance from exact laws, measured bounds and error budgets. Do not try to derive transistor behaviour from QED, Kaluza–Klein, DFT or similar; section 4 says why that was ruled out.

## 3. Working rules

These follow from the spec. Apply them unless the owner says otherwise.

1. Tag every hypothesis with exactly one of LAW, MODEL, MEAS, ENV, THM or TCB (spec, "Assumption tags"). Put the tag in the docstring of the structure field or hypothesis.
2. State physical and modelling assumptions as explicit hypotheses or structure fields, never as global `axiom`s. `#print axioms` on each main theorem should list only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
3. `bv_decide` and `native_decide` add `Lean.ofReduceBool`, which puts the Lean compiler in the trusted base. Use them only in bit-level datapath lemmas, and list each use.
4. Nothing counts as done while it contains `sorry`. Each milestone ends in sorry-free theorems.
5. Prove the spec's statements as written. If one is false or unprovable, do not quietly weaken it. Record the counterexample or the missing hypothesis in the change log (section 6) and tell the owner, who maintains the living spec.
6. Build non-vacuity witnesses. For each bundle of hypotheses (device contracts with the parameter sheet, U_ENV, H_cells), construct a concrete instance that satisfies it. The review already caught one missing condition, K9: without it the contracts and K5–K6 cannot hold together, so every theorem would be vacuously true. A toy witness for the device layer is in section 5.
7. Keep the core dimensionless: plain ℝ, with V_DD = 1 or explicit SI constants, and no units library.
8. Define trajectories on intervals [a, b] with one-sided derivatives (`IsTrajOn`), never on all of ℝ.

## 4. Decisions already made

Treat these as settled unless the owner reopens them.

**Why not first principles.** At chip energies QED reduces to the Coulomb many-body Schrödinger equation, but no chain of theorems leads from there to a transistor:

- the crystallization conjecture (that matter settles into lattices) is still largely open, so band theory has to assume the lattice;
- rigorous derivations of irreversible transport reach a rarefied hard-sphere gas (Deng–Hani–Ma, 2025) and one non-interacting electron in a weak random potential (Erdős–Salmhofer–Yau), not interacting electrons in a device;
- computing the exact exchange–correlation functional of density functional theory is QMA-hard (Schuch–Verstraete, 2009), and spectral gaps are undecidable in general (Cubitt–Pérez-García–Wolf, 2015);
- a real device's atomic microstate (dopant positions, interface traps) is unknown, so the strongest true statements are about measured ensembles.

**Kaluza–Klein** gets the U(1) gauge structure from 5-D geometry but cannot produce the electron. KK charge obeys |q|/m ≤ √(16πG) in Heaviside–Lorentz units, and the electron exceeds that bound by about 10²¹, so it has to be added by hand. Below QED it changes nothing.

**Gauge symmetry on ℝ⁴** gives only the metric-free part of electromagnetism: Faraday's law, ∇·B = 0 and charge conservation, but not the vacuum law that fixes Coulomb's law. That part is exactly what Kirchhoff's laws need, which is why L0 uses nothing else.

**Design decisions in the spec**

- The lowest layer is L0: potentials (φ, A), charge conservation and the lumped-matter discipline give KCL and KVL. Gauss's and Ampère's laws are never used; capacitances enter as measured bounds.
- Devices are contracts (inequalities), not compact-model formulas. Passivity comes from the second law on a quasi-static channel model (LAW + MODEL). Output monotonicity is device-specific (MEAS), since a tunnel diode violates it.
- The circuit model is an explicit ODE, not a DAE. Every state node has grounded, Lipschitz, nonlinear capacitance with C_k ≥ C_k^min > 0. Coupling capacitance is folded in with Miller factors 0 to 2, and residual crosstalk goes to the fidelity budget.
- Deterministic core, probabilistic shell. Physics, timing and refinement are deterministic theorems. Probability only bounds how often their hypotheses fail (G_init, G_mfg, MF_k), combined by the union bound with no independence assumption. MF_k re-anchors the model every cycle.
- The ISA source of truth is the Sail RISC-V model, translated to Lean. v0 halts on traps, so program specs must imply ¬halted.
- The v0 target is a single-cycle RV32I-subset core built from static CMOS INV, NAND2 and NOR2 cells and positive-edge D flip-flops, with flip-flop memory and one clock domain. Each flip-flop's clock pin is an ideal ENV waveform.
- The Buridan theorem (L3.3) is an early sanity check: it proves that setup and hold cannot be removed.
- The L0 bridge is formalized last (M7). Nothing above it depends on its proof, only on its statement.

## 7. Spec snapshot: Assembly-to-Physics Verification Spec

Verbatim copy of the living spec at doc rev 46 (2026-10-24). Two edits only: the layer diagram is redrawn as text, and headings sit one level deeper.

### Purpose

This spec defines machine-checkable refinement theorems that connect an assembly program to a circuit-level physical model of the chip that runs it. Every hypothesis carries a tag saying what justifies it.

The end result is one theorem of this shape:

> With probability at least 1 − (ε_init + ε_mfg + N·ε_cycle), a chip built from the verified netlist and run within its operating envelope stays on track: its register voltages encode ISA.run k s₀ at every sampling instant k ≤ N. The probability is over manufacturing variation, program loading and runtime disturbances, and the intended initial state s₀ satisfies Pre(s₀). In particular, Post(ISA.run N s₀) is physically realized.

Design principles:

1. **Deterministic core, probabilistic shell.** Physics, timing and refinement theorems are deterministic statements about a model. Probability only measures how often the model's hypotheses hold for a given chip and run.
2. **Contracts, not formulas.** Devices are specified by inequalities. Theorems quantify over every device function meeting its contract, so they apply to the real devices whatever their exact curves.
3. **Every hypothesis is tagged.** Each is an exact law, a modeling assumption, a measured bound, an operating condition, a theorem or a trusted tool.
4. **Exact laws carry the weight.** Only charge conservation, Faraday's law (through potentials) and the second law (as passivity) are used. Gauss's and Ampère's laws enter only through measured capacitances.

Notation: Bool = {0, 1}. Voltages are in volts, currents in amperes, times in seconds. V_DD is the supply voltage, constant during a run.

### Assumption tags

Every hypothesis carries exactly one tag, and the tags together define the trust boundary.

| Tag | Meaning | Justified by | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LAW | Exact physical law, taken as an axiom | Physics | Charge conservation; E and B from potentials; passivity (second law) |
| MODEL | Modeling hypothesis with a stated validity condition | A LAW plus a physical condition, validated by measurement | Lumped-matter discipline; capacitance laws; cell-library hypothesis H_cells (v0); per-cycle fidelity and its ε_cycle bound |
| MEAS | Numerical bound with a stated confidence | Measurement, characterization, process statistics | I_off, G_on, capacitance ranges, ε_init, ε_mfg |
| ENV | Operating condition the user must ensure | Board, supply, clock source, loader | V_DD range, clock waveforms and edge spacing, input timing |
| THM | Statement proved in Lean | Proof | Rail invariance, cell delays, synchronous refinement |
| TCB | Trusted tool or translation | Audit, cross-validation | Lean kernel, netlist extraction, Sail-to-Lean translation |

A MEAS bound is stated over an ensemble (process corners and the temperature range). Its failure probability is charged to the probabilistic shell, never dropped silently.

### Layer overview

The stack has seven layers joined by theorems, plus one probabilistic shell that ties the physical chip to the model. v0 targets a single-cycle RV32I-subset core built from static CMOS cells (INV, NAND2, NOR2) and positive-edge D flip-flops, with flip-flop memory and one clock domain.

```text
L5  ISA and core ......... RV32I-subset step; single-cycle core; abs and enc
     ▲ THM L5.1: the core refines the ISA
```

The shell's hypotheses attach at L1 (device contracts), L2 (the per-cycle fidelity of the circuit model) and L4 (the invariant each cycle re-anchors at).

### L0 — Physical laws and the lumped model

Kirchhoff's laws, and with them the nodal ODE of L2, are derived from charge conservation and potentials under explicit lumped-matter hypotheses. No other electromagnetism is used, and the layers above use only the conclusions.

**Objects**

- Space ℝ³ (EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)) and time ℝ.
- Potentials φ : ℝ → ℝ³ → ℝ and A : ℝ → ℝ³ → ℝ³, both C².
- Sources ρ : ℝ → ℝ³ → ℝ and J : ℝ → ℝ³ → ℝ³, both C¹.
- Derived fields E := −∇φ − ∂ₜA and B := ∇ × A.

**Laws**

- LAW-1 (charge conservation): ∂ₜρ + ∇·J = 0.
- LAW-2 (homogeneous Maxwell): ∇·B = 0 and ∇ × E = −∂ₜB hold by construction (THM, from symmetry of second derivatives). Taking potentials as primitive loses no generality on ℝ⁴.
- LAW-3 (second law): used only through the passivity clause of the device contracts (L1).
- Gauss's and Ampère's laws are not used. They determine capacitances, which enter as MEAS bounds.

**Lumped realization (MODEL)**

A circuit has nodes V, one of them ground, and elements; element b has a set T_b of distinct terminal nodes. A lumped realization consists of:

- node regions N_k: pairwise disjoint closed axis-aligned boxes, with reference points p_k ∈ N_k;
- element regions D_b: finite unions of boxes, pairwise interior-disjoint, each meeting N_k in a cross-section S_{b,k} for k ∈ T_b and disjoint from every other node region;
- terminal currents i_{b,k}(t) := flux of J(t) from N_k into D_b through S_{b,k}.

Multi-terminal elements reduce to two-terminal branches. Pick a reference terminal r_b of each element, and make each other terminal k ∈ T_b a branch β = (b, k) from k to r_b carrying i_β := i_{b,k}. Each branch gets a piecewise-C¹ path γ_β from p_k to p_{r_b}. The branches form a directed multigraph G = (V, Br), assumed connected, with incidence matrix A(k, β) = +1 if k = tail(β), −1 if k = head(β), and 0 otherwise.

Definitions:

- Branch voltage v_β(t) := ∫ over γ_β of E(t)·dl.
- Circulations: integer vectors c ∈ ℤ^Br with A·c = 0. For each, Γ_c(t) := Σ_β c_β ∫ over γ_β of A(t)·dl. Γ_c is gauge-invariant; it is the magnetic flux through any surface spanning the closed chain.

Lumped-matter discipline:

- LMD-1 (no stray flux): Γ_c is constant in t for every circulation c.
- LMD-2 (confined current): J·n = 0 on every ∂N_k and ∂D_b except on the cross-sections.
- LMD-3 (neutral elements): the net charge in each D_b is constant in t. Charge that an element stores, such as gate and channel charge, is attributed to node capacitance instead (L1).
- LMD-4 (capacitive nodes): at each state node k, the charge Q_k(t) := ∫ over N_k of ρ(t) obeys Q_k = q_k(e_k). Here e_k is the node potential of THM L0.3, and q_k′ = C_k is the node's capacitance law (L1). Boundary nodes are unconstrained, since their sources supply whatever charge flows.

**Theorems**

- THM L0.1 (KVL, exact form): Σ_β c_β·v_β(t) = −dΓ_c/dt for every circulation c, so LMD-1 gives KVL. Proof: the gradient term telescopes to Σ_k φ(p_k)·(A·c)_k = 0, and the A term is differentiated under the integral.
- THM L0.2 (KCL, exact form): under LMD-2 and LMD-3, Σ_β A(k, β)·i_β(t) = −dQ_k/dt for every node k, and each element's reference terminal carries minus the sum of its other terminal currents. Proof: LAW-1, differentiation under the integral, and the divergence theorem on boxes (in Mathlib).
- THM L0.3 (node potentials): if v is orthogonal to every circulation, there is e : V → ℝ with v = Aᵀe, unique once e(ground) = 0. This is linear algebra: (ker A)^⊥ = range Aᵀ.
- THM L0.4 (bridge to L2): assume LMD-1 to LMD-4, each FET's channel current given by its device function, and zero gate and body current (L1). Then the node potentials satisfy the L2 ODE C_k(e_k)·ė_k = F_k(t, e). Proof: THM L0.3 gives e, and THM L0.2 with dQ_k/dt = C_k(e_k)·ė_k gives the equation.

The exact forms price each hypothesis. KVL fails by exactly the EMF of stray flux, and KCL by exactly the stray charge accumulation. Both residuals are charged to the fidelity hypothesis in the probabilistic shell.

### L1 — Device contracts and node capacitance

Devices and node capacitances enter only through contracts: inequalities whose clauses are tagged by what justifies them. Every theorem above L1 holds for every device function and capacitance law that satisfies them.

**Node capacitance** (law MODEL, bounds MEAS)

- Each state node k has a charge law q_k with capacitance C_k := q_k′, Lipschitz and valued in [C_k^min, C_k^max], with C_k^min > 0. Gate, junction, wire and device-internal charge storage are lumped here.
- C_k^min counts coupling capacitance with Miller factor 0, so only capacitance to fixed potentials remains. C_k^max counts it with Miller factor 2.
- Crosstalk outside that range is charged to the fidelity hypothesis MF_k. Examples are a faster aggressor switching the same way, or a glitch from one switching the opposite way.

**Sources and gates**

- Ideal voltage sources are boundary nodes: ground (0 V), supply (V_DD), one clock pin per flip-flop, and primary inputs.
- MOSFET gate and body currents are zero (MODEL; gate leakage is charged to MF_k).

**FET contract**

A device function I(v_g, v_d, v_s) gives the quasi-static channel current from drain to source. Write v_lo := min(v_d, v_s), v_hi := max(v_d, v_s), v_ds := v_d − v_s and Rail := [0, V_DD^max].

| Clause | Statement | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 Passivity | v_ds · I(v_g, v_d, v_s) ≥ 0 | LAW (second law), applied to a quasi-static channel model (MODEL) |
| C2 Regularity | I is globally Lipschitz with constant L_I, and globally bounded | MODEL |
| C3 Off leakage | if off(v_g, v_d, v_s) and v_d, v_s ∈ Rail, then abs(I) ≤ I_off | MEAS |
| C4 On drive | if on(v_g, v_d, v_s) and v_d, v_s ∈ Rail, then abs(I) ≥ min(G_on·abs(v_ds), I_sat) | MEAS |
| C5 Output monotonicity | I is nondecreasing in v_d and nonincreasing in v_s | MEAS |
| C6 Bound | abs(I) ≤ I_max whenever v_g, v_d, v_s ∈ Rail | MEAS |

The on and off conditions depend on polarity:

| Polarity | on | off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| n | v_g − v_lo ≥ V_on | v_g − v_lo ≤ V_off |
| p | v_hi − v_g ≥ V_on | v_hi − v_g ≤ V_off |

Each polarity has its own V_on, V_off, I_off, G_on, I_sat, I_max and L_I, valid over the stated supply and temperature range, with V_off < V_on (K9). Notes:

- Without K9, some gate voltage would make a device both on and off. C3 and C4 would then contradict each other, no device could meet the contract, and every theorem would hold vacuously.
- Derived: I(v_g, v, v) = 0 (THM, from C1 and the continuity in C2).
- C2 constrains the model outside the rails too. That is harmless, because rail invariance (THM L2.3) keeps every trajectory inside them.
- C5 is a property of this device type, not of physics: a tunnel diode violates it while satisfying C1.

```lean
  L : NNReal

def onCond : Polarity → FETParams → ℝ → ℝ → ℝ → Prop
  | .n, P, g, d, s => g - min d s ≥ P.Von
  | .p, P, g, d, s => max d s - g ≥ P.Von

def offCond : Polarity → FETParams → ℝ → ℝ → ℝ → Prop
  | .n, P, g, d, s => g - min d s ≤ P.Voff
  | .p, P, g, d, s => max d s - g ≤ P.Voff

structure FETContract (pol : Polarity) (P : FETParams) (Vmax : ℝ)
    (I : ℝ → ℝ → ℝ → ℝ) : Prop where
  passive   : ∀ g d s, 0 ≤ (d - s) * I g d s                                   -- LAW + MODEL
  lipschitz : LipschitzWith P.L (fun x : ℝ × ℝ × ℝ => I x.1 x.2.1 x.2.2)        -- MODEL
  bdd       : ∃ B, ∀ g d s, |I g d s| ≤ B                                      -- MODEL
  off_leak  : ∀ g d s, d ∈ Set.Icc 0 Vmax → s ∈ Set.Icc 0 Vmax →
                offCond pol P g d s → |I g d s| ≤ P.Ioff                       -- MEAS
  on_drive  : ∀ g d s, d ∈ Set.Icc 0 Vmax → s ∈ Set.Icc 0 Vmax →
                onCond pol P g d s → min (P.Gon * |d - s|) P.Isat ≤ |I g d s|  -- MEAS
  mono_d    : ∀ g s, Monotone (fun d => I g d s)                               -- MEAS
  anti_s    : ∀ g d, Antitone (fun s => I g d s)                               -- MEAS
  bounded   : ∀ g d s, g ∈ Set.Icc 0 Vmax → d ∈ Set.Icc 0 Vmax →
                s ∈ Set.Icc 0 Vmax → |I g d s| ≤ P.Imax                        -- MEAS

structure NodeCap (Cmin Cmax : ℝ) (C : ℝ → ℝ) : Prop where
  pos       : 0 < Cmin
  range     : ∀ v, C v ∈ Set.Icc Cmin Cmax                                     -- MEAS
  lipschitz : ∃ K, LipschitzWith K C                                           -- MODEL
```

The consistency conditions that tie these parameters to logic levels (K0–K12) are in the parameter sheet.

### L2 — Circuit model

The chip is modeled as a nodal ODE: one state per non-boundary node, grounded nonlinear capacitance, and channel currents from the device functions. THM L0.4 derives it from L0, and every theorem here is stated on a time interval [a, b].

**Netlist**

- Boundary nodes j ∈ Fin m with prescribed potentials u : ℝ → (Fin m → ℝ): ground, supply, one clock pin per flip-flop, and primary inputs. The environment predicate U_ENV (L4) fixes which waveforms are allowed.
- State nodes k ∈ Fin n with capacitance laws C_k (L1).
- FETs: each has a polarity, parameters, a device function satisfying its contract, and gate, drain and source terminals, each a state or boundary node.
- Net channel current into state node k: F_k(t, e) := Σ of I over FETs with source at k, minus Σ of I over FETs with drain at k, each I evaluated at its terminals' potentials.

**Dynamics**

```latex
C_k\big(e_k(t)\big)\,\dot e_k(t) = F_k\big(t, e(t)\big), \qquad k \in \mathrm{Fin}\, n
```

**Lean sketch** (not compiled)

```lean
inductive Node (n m : ℕ) | state (k : Fin n) | bdry (j : Fin m)
  deriving DecidableEq

structure FET (n m : ℕ) where
  pol : Polarity
  P   : FETParams
  I   : ℝ → ℝ → ℝ → ℝ
  g d s : Node n m

structure Netlist (n m : ℕ) where
  C     : Fin n → ℝ → ℝ              -- capacitance law of each state node
  C_pos : ∀ k v, 0 < C k v
  fets  : List (FET n m)

variable {n m : ℕ}

def pot (u : Fin m → ℝ) (e : Fin n → ℝ) : Node n m → ℝ
  | .state k => e k
  | .bdry j  => u j

/-- Net channel current into state node `k` (drain-to-source convention). -/
def Netlist.F (N : Netlist n m) (u : Fin m → ℝ) (e : Fin n → ℝ) (k : Fin n) : ℝ :=
  (N.fets.map fun f =>
     let i := f.I (pot u e f.g) (pot u e f.d) (pot u e f.s)
     (if f.s = .state k then i else 0) - (if f.d = .state k then i else 0)).sum

/-- A trajectory on [a, b]: continuous there, with the ODE as a right derivative on [a, b). -/
def Netlist.IsTrajOn (N : Netlist n m) (u : ℝ → Fin m → ℝ)
    (e : ℝ → Fin n → ℝ) (a b : ℝ) : Prop :=
  ContinuousOn e (Set.Icc a b) ∧
  ∀ t ∈ Set.Ico a b,
    HasDerivWithinAt e (fun k => N.F (u t) (e t) k / N.C k (e t k)) (Set.Ici t) t
```

**Theorems** (each for any a ≤ b)

- THM L2.1 (Tellegen): for any graph, if v = Aᵀe and A·i = 0, then v·i = 0.
- THM L2.2 (well-posedness): suppose u is continuous, every device satisfies C2, and every C_k is Lipschitz with values in [C_k^min, C_k^max]. Then each state e(a) has a unique trajectory on [a, b]. Proof: F/C is continuous in t and globally Lipschitz in e, since C2 makes F bounded as well as Lipschitz. Picard–Lindelöf applies.
- THM L2.3 (rail invariance): suppose every device satisfies C1 and C2, every boundary node that touches a channel stays in [0, V_DD], and e(a) ∈ [0, V_DD]^n. Then e(t) ∈ [0, V_DD]^n on [a, b]; gate-only boundary nodes are unconstrained. Proof: on the face e_k = V_DD with every other node in [0, V_DD], C1 and I(v_g, v, v) = 0 make each channel current at k flow out, so F_k ≤ 0. Symmetrically F_k ≥ 0 on e_k = 0. The field points into the box, and Gronwall on the distance to the box finishes.
- THM L2.4 (continuous dependence): e(b) depends Lipschitz-continuously on e(a) and on u in the sup norm over [a, b] (Gronwall).
- THM L2.5 (slew bound): if all boundary potentials lie in Rail, then abs(ė_k(t)) ≤ n_k·I_max / C_k^min, where n_k is the number of channel terminals at node k. Uses C6 and THM L2.3.

Rail invariance needs all capacitance to be grounded. With node-to-node coupling capacitance the theorem is false, and so is the physics: real outputs briefly overshoot the rails. That is why v0 folds coupling into C_k and charges the residual to the fidelity hypothesis.

### L3 — Cells and the digital abstraction

Logic levels are voltage intervals, and each cell carries two theorems. Valid inputs give a valid output within t_pd, and a valid output stays readable for t_cd whatever the inputs do. The flip-flop adds setup and hold, and the Buridan theorem proves they cannot be removed.

**Logic levels** (MEAS/design)

- Thresholds 0 < V_OL < V_IL < V_IH < V_OH < V_DD^min.
- Noise margins NM_L := V_IL − V_OL > 0 and NM_H := V_OH − V_IH > 0.
- validIn(0, v) :⇔ v ≤ V_IL, and validIn(1, v) :⇔ v ≥ V_IH.
- validOut(0, v) :⇔ v ≤ V_OL, and validOut(1, v) :⇔ v ≥ V_OH. validOut(b, v) implies validIn(b, v).

**Combinational cells**

The v0 library is INV, NAND2 and NOR2. A cell is a sub-netlist with input pins, one output node, possibly internal nodes, and a Boolean function f. Its output capacitance law has range [C_out^min, C_out^max], fanout load included. In static CMOS the output node touches only the cell's own channels and the gates of its fanout.

- THM L3.1 (propagation): suppose each input j satisfies validIn(x_j, u_j(t)) for all t ∈ [t₀, t₁], and the cell's nodes lie in [0, V_DD] at t₀. Then (a) validOut(f(x), e_out(t)) for all t ∈ [t₀ + t_pd, t₁], and (b) once validOut(f(x), e_out) holds at some t₂ ∈ [t₀, t₁], it holds on all of [t₂, t₁].
- THM L3.2 (contamination): if validOut(b, e_out(t₁)), then validIn(b, e_out(t)) for all t ∈ [t₁, t₁ + t_cd], whatever the inputs do within the rails. Here t_cd := min(NM_L, NM_H)·C_out^min / (n_out·I_max), with n_out the number of channels at the output. Proof: THM L2.5.

**Inverter bounds** (THM, from the contracts and K1–K6)

Rising output, input valid low: the n-FET is off (K1) and leaks at most I_off^n, and the p-FET is on (K2). While e_out < V_OH:

```latex
C_{out}(e_{out})\,\dot e_{out} \;\ge\; \min\!\big(G_{on}^{p}\,(V_{DD}^{min}-V_{OH}),\; I_{sat}^{p}\big) - I_{off}^{n} \;=:\; I_{rise} \;>\; 0 \quad (\mathrm{K5})
```

Falling output, input valid high: the p-FET is off (K4) and the n-FET is on (K3). While e_out > V_OL:

```latex
C_{out}(e_{out})\,\dot e_{out} \;\le\; -\Big(\min\!\big(G_{on}^{n}\,V_{OL},\; I_{sat}^{n}\big) - I_{off}^{p}\Big) \;=:\; -I_{fall} \;<\; 0 \quad (\mathrm{K6})
```

Hence t_pd(INV) := C_out^max · max(V_OH / I_rise, (V_DD^max − V_OL) / I_fall). Clause (b) of THM L3.1 holds because the derivative keeps its sign at the threshold.

NAND2 and NOR2 get the same two theorems. In v0 they are part of the cell-library hypothesis H_cells (below). The v1 proof uses a series-stack lemma and the comparison principle for cooperative systems, which C5 makes available. It must let the internal stack node start anywhere in the rails (charge sharing), and it needs the stack conditions K10 and K11.

**Clock pins** (ENV)

The run has actual clock edge times t₀ < t₁ < … with t_{k+1} − t_k ≥ T_clk. They need not lie on a fixed grid, so oscillator drift is allowed. Flip-flop r's clock pin is its own boundary node, and its k-th edge t_k^r lies in [t_k, t_k + s_max]. ClockOK_r holds when, for every k:

- CLK_r(t) ≤ V_IL on [t_k^r − t_low, t_k^r];
- CLK_r(t) ≥ V_IH on [t_k^r + t_slew, t_k^r + t_slew + t_high];
- CLK_r(t) ∈ [0, V_DD] at all times.

**Flip-flop contract** (part of H_cells: MODEL in v0, THM in v1 from a transistor-level master–slave netlist)

Assume ClockOK_r, and D(t) ∈ [0, V_DD] at all times. There are sets Hold(d, t) of internal states, for d ∈ Bool, that may depend on time through the clock waveform. In a master–slave flip-flop they require both latches to hold d while the clock is high, and only the slave while it is low. No time-independent set works, because each latch is transparent in one phase. Then:

- FF1 (hold): if the internal state is in Hold(d, s) at some s ∈ [t_k^r + t_cq^max, t_{k+1}^r), it lies in Hold(d, t) for every t ∈ [s, t_{k+1}^r), and validOut(d, Q(t)) for all t ∈ [s, t_{k+1}^r + t_cq^min].
- FF2 (capture): if the internal state is in Hold(d, s) at some s ∈ [t_k^r + t_cq^max, t_{k+1}^r − t_su], and validIn(d′, D(t)) for all t ∈ [t_{k+1}^r − t_su, t_{k+1}^r + t_h], then at τ := t_{k+1}^r + t_cq^max the internal state is in Hold(d′, τ).

The sets Hold(d, t) are the invariant tubes a transistor-level proof will construct. The state-based form is what lets the probabilistic shell re-anchor the model every cycle.

- THM L3.3 (Buridan: setup and hold cannot be removed). Take any L2 netlist that satisfies FF1–FF2, with D and a ClockOK clock as boundary inputs and its state at t_k^r + t_cq^max in Hold(d₀, t_k^r + t_cq^max). Let D_τ be 0 until τ and then rise continuously to V_DD over a fixed time t_r, where t_k^r + t_cq^max + t_r ≤ t_{k+1}^r − t_su. Fix an observation time t_obs ∈ [t_{k+1}^r + t_cq^max, t_{k+2}^r + t_cq^min]. Then some τ* gives Q(t_obs) = (V_IL + V_IH)/2, which is not even a valid input level.
  - Proof: τ = t_k^r + t_cq^max keeps D valid 1 through the window, so Q(t_obs) ≥ V_OH by FF2 and FF1. τ = t_{k+1}^r + t_h keeps D valid 0 through the window, so Q(t_obs) ≤ V_OL. The map τ ↦ Q(t_obs) is continuous by THM L2.4, and the intermediate value theorem gives τ*.

**Cell-library hypothesis H_cells**

Fix any device functions that satisfy their contracts and any capacitance laws within their ranges. Then every cell instance of the verified netlist meets its contract:

- every INV satisfies THM L3.1–L3.2 (a theorem, above);
- every NAND2 and NOR2 satisfies them with its listed t_pd and t_cd (MODEL in v0);
- every flip-flop satisfies FF1–FF2 with its Hold sets and t_su, t_h, t_cq^min, t_cq^max (MODEL in v0).

v1 turns the last two items into theorems.

**Composition**

- THM L3.4 (combinational timing, per node): take a DAG of cells whose nodes start in [0, V_DD] and whose inputs stay in the rails. Suppose each DAG input i is validIn(x_i) on [a(i), b(i)]. For each cell output n with inputs In(n), set a(n) := max over In(n) of a, plus t_pd(n), and b(n) := min over In(n) of b, plus t_cd(n). Then every node n is validIn(val(n)) on [a(n), b(n)], where val is the DAG's Boolean value, provided a(m) ≤ min over In(m) of b for every cell m in n's fan-in cone. Proof: induction in topological order, applying THM L3.1 on [max a, min b] and then THM L3.2.

### L4 — Synchronous system

A gate-level netlist with registers is a Mealy machine, and the circuit model implements it cycle by cycle when every path meets setup and hold. This is the classic digital abstraction, proved rather than assumed.

**Objects**

- Registers R, primary inputs PI, primary outputs PO, and a combinational DAG of cells from the Q pins and PI to the D pins and PO.
- Boolean semantics: δ : Bool^R × Bool^PI → Bool^R and λ : Bool^R × Bool^PI → Bool^PO, by composing cell functions along the DAG.
- Mealy machine M := (δ, λ), with run σ_{k+1} := δ(σ_k, x_k).
- Sampling instants s_k := t_k + s_max + t_cq^max, on the run's actual clock edges (L3).
- Invariant Inv_L4(σ) on model states at s_k: every flip-flop r has its internal state in Hold(σ_r, s_k), and every node lies in [0, V_DD].

**Environment predicate U_ENV** (ENV)

A boundary waveform u belongs to U_ENV for the edge times t₀ < t₁ < … when:

- u is continuous, ground is 0, and the supply is a constant V_DD ∈ [V_DD^min, V_DD^max];
- every boundary node, clock pins and primary inputs included, stays in [0, V_DD];
- t_{k+1} − t_k ≥ T_clk, and every flip-flop clock pin satisfies ClockOK_r for these edges;
- each PI is validIn(x_k) on [t_k + t_in, t_{k+1} + s_max + t_h].

**Timing constraints** (finite checks over the netlist's paths, closed by norm_num)

- K8 (strict): t_cq^max + t_su + s_max < T_clk, so s_k falls inside FF1's half-open window.
- Setup, for every path p from a Q pin to a D pin: t_cq^max + Σ_{c ∈ p} t_pd(c) + t_su + s_max ≤ T_clk. For a path from a primary input: t_in + Σ_{c ∈ p} t_pd(c) + t_su ≤ T_clk.
- Hold, for every path p from a Q pin to a D pin: t_cq^min + Σ_{c ∈ p} t_cd(c) ≥ t_h + s_max.

These per-path checks are exactly what THM L3.4's per-node arrival times need to place each D pin's window around its capture edge.

**Theorem**

- THM L4.1 (synchronous refinement): assume H_cells, K8, setup and hold on every path, u ∈ U_ENV, and Inv_L4(σ_k) at s_k. Then:
  - Inv_L4(δ(σ_k, x_k)) holds at s_{k+1};
  - every Q pin r is validOut((σ\_k)\_r) on \[s\_k, t\_{k+1} + t\_cq^min\];
  - every PO is validIn(λ(σ_k, x_k)) on its THM L3.4 window, when THM L3.4's window condition holds on its fan-in cone.
  - Proof: FF1 holds each Q from s_k. Feed THM L3.4 the Q windows [s_k, t_{k+1} + t_cq^min] and the PI windows from U_ENV. Setup makes each D pin validIn(δ(σ_k, x_k)) by t_{k+1}^r − t_su, and hold keeps it so through t_{k+1}^r + t_h. FF2 captures, and FF1 carries Hold to s_{k+1}.
- Corollary: from Inv_L4(σ₀) at s₀, the model's register voltages are validOut(σ_k) at every s_k, where σ_k is the Mealy run.

### L5 — ISA and core

A single-cycle core refines the ISA: decoding the register bits after one clock cycle gives exactly ISA.step of the decoded state.

**ISA (v0 subset of RV32I)**

- ArchState: pc : BitVec 32; regs : Fin 32 → BitVec 32 with regs 0 = 0; mem : BitVec 32 → BitVec 8; halted : Bool.
- Memory window [MemBase, MemBase + MemSize), with MemBase + MemSize ≤ 2³². WellFormed s :⇔ s.mem is zero outside the window.
- step : ArchState → ArchState is total, and a halted state steps to itself. A trap sets halted and changes nothing else. Traps are illegal or unsupported instructions, misaligned accesses or jump targets, and any access outside the window. THM: step preserves WellFormed.
- Instructions: LUI, AUIPC, JAL, JALR; BEQ, BNE, BLT, BGE, BLTU, BGEU; LB, LH, LW, LBU, LHU; SB, SH, SW; ADDI, SLTI, SLTIU, XORI, ORI, ANDI, SLLI, SRLI, SRAI; ADD, SUB, SLL, SLT, SLTU, XOR, SRL, SRA, OR, AND.
- Excluded: FENCE, ECALL, EBREAK, CSR instructions, interrupts and privilege modes.
- Source of truth (TCB): the official Sail RISC-V model, translated to Lean, with its configuration pinned (including misaligned-access handling). THM: whenever the hand-written step does not trap on s, it agrees with the Sail-derived step on s. Where v0 halts, Sail would jump to a trap handler, so program specs must exclude halting (L6).

**Core**

- A synchronous netlist (L4) with no primary inputs. Its registers hold pc (32 bits), x1–x31 (31 × 32 bits), the memory window (MemSize × 8 bits, a flip-flop array holding code and data) and halted (1 bit).
- abs : Bool^R → ArchState reads the architectural fields off the register bits, with memory zero outside the window. enc : ArchState → Bool^R satisfies abs(enc s) = s for WellFormed s, and enc(abs σ) = σ.
- THM L5.1 (single-cycle refinement): abs(δ(σ)) = step(abs(σ)) for every σ.
- Datapath lemmas (ALU, shifter, comparator, decoder) are bit-level and suit `decide`, `simp` or `bv_decide`. `bv_decide` adds the Lean compiler to the TCB, because its proofs use Lean.ofReduceBool.

### L6 — Programs

A program is a list of instructions placed in memory, and its specification is a pre/postcondition on ISA runs. This layer is ordinary program verification against ISA.step.

- Encoding: encInstr : Instr → BitVec 32 for the v0 subset, with THM decode(encInstr i) = some i. The assembler is encInstr mapped over the program, so no separate assembler is trusted.
- Loading: loaded P s :⇔ the bytes of P sit at address base in s.mem, and s.pc = base.
- Halting convention: a program ends in jal x0, 0. That self-loop leaves the state unchanged, so Post can be checked at any cycle from N on.
- Program spec (THM, per program): ∀ s, WellFormed s → Pre s → loaded P s → Post (run N s), where run N iterates step N times and Post implies halted = false. Requiring ¬halted rules out a spec that a trap satisfies, where Sail would have jumped to a handler instead.

### Probabilistic shell and the end-to-end theorem

The physical chip enters the formalization only through its loading, its device contracts and one fidelity hypothesis per cycle. Probability bounds how often those fail, and the union bound combines the failures without any independence assumption.

**Physical interface**

(Ω, μ) is a probability space over manufactured chips, program loading and runtime disturbances. The physical world is seen only through four fields of each run ω:

- devs(ω): the actual device functions of the chip's FETs;
- caps(ω): the actual node capacitance laws;
- edges(ω): the actual clock edge times t_k, which ENV requires to be at least T_clk apart;
- y_k(ω): the physical register-node voltages at the sampling instant s_k(ω).

dig(y) is the bit vector read from y with thresholds V_IL and V_IH. It is undefined if any component lies strictly between them.

**Events**

- G_init: dig(y₀(ω)) = enc(s₀). MEAS: μ(G_initᶜ) ≤ ε_init.
- G_mfg: every device function in devs(ω) satisfies its contract, and every law in caps(ω) lies in its range. MEAS: μ(G_mfgᶜ) ≤ ε_mfg.
- MF_k(η), per-cycle fidelity: suppose σ := dig(y_{k−1}(ω)) is defined. Then there are a waveform u ∈ U_ENV on edges(ω) and a trajectory e of the verified netlist on [s_{k−1}, s_k], built from devs(ω) and caps(ω) and driven by u. The trajectory starts in Inv_L4(σ) at s_{k−1}, and each component of y_k(ω) lies within η of the matching register node of e(s_k).
- MODEL: μ(MF_k(η)ᶜ ∩ G_mfg) ≤ ε_cycle for every k. This is MODEL rather than MEAS because MF_k quantifies over model trajectories, which no instrument observes. It is estimated from noise analysis and measured upset rates.

MF_k is where every unmodeled effect lives: thermal noise, crosstalk outside the Miller range, inductive noise, IR drop, clock jitter beyond s_max, LMD residuals and particle strikes. Requiring u ∈ U_ENV on the chip's own edges is what makes a dead or misbehaving clock count as a fidelity failure. Re-anchoring every cycle, instead of tracking one trajectory for the whole run, keeps the hypothesis plausible, because digital circuits restore their levels every cycle.

**Theorems**

- THM Core (deterministic): assume H_cells, K0–K12, setup and hold on every path, the program spec (L6), WellFormed s₀, Pre(s₀) and loaded P s₀. Then every ω ∈ G_init ∩ G_mfg with MF_k(η) for k = 1, …, N has dig(y_k(ω)) = enc(run k s₀) for every k ≤ N, and Post holds of abs(dig(y_N(ω))).
  - Proof, by induction on k: MF_k supplies a model trajectory from Inv_L4(enc(run (k−1) s₀)). THM L4.1 makes its registers validOut at s_k, encoding δ of that state. Since η < min(NM_L, NM_H) (K7), the physical voltages stay on the correct side of V_IL and V_IH, so dig reads the same bits. THM L5.1 identifies δ with step, and the program spec gives Post.
- THM End-to-end: μ{ω | ∃ k ≤ N, dig(y_k(ω)) ≠ enc(run k s₀)} ≤ ε_init + ε_mfg + N·ε_cycle.
  - Proof: pure measure theory. By THM Core, the failure set lies inside G_initᶜ ∪ G_mfgᶜ ∪ ⋃_k (MF_k(η)ᶜ ∩ G_mfg), and the union bound finishes.
  - μ is applied as an outer measure, so no measurability is needed. "Succeeds with probability at least 1 − ε" is then the inner-measure reading.

### Trusted computing base

Everything below is trusted rather than proved. Listing it in the spec means it can only shrink deliberately.

- Lean 4 kernel and Mathlib, with the standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound).
- The Lean compiler, only if `bv_decide` or `native_decide` is used (Lean.ofReduceBool).
- The Sail RISC-V model's fidelity to the RISC-V specification, its pinned configuration, and its translation to Lean.
- Netlist and parasitic extraction: the claim that the fabricated chip has the transistor netlist and capacitance ranges in the model.
- The measurements and statistics behind every MEAS bound, including ε_init and ε_mfg.
- The MODEL hypotheses: the lumped-matter discipline, H_cells in v0, and the fidelity hypothesis MF_k(η) with its ε_cycle bound, the single place where unmodeled physics is assumed away.
- The ENV conditions: supply range, temperature range, clock waveforms (ClockOK, edges at least T_clk apart) and input timing.

### Parameter sheet and consistency conditions

Every number the theorems depend on lives here, with a unit and a tag. Once values are filled in, the consistency conditions are inequalities Lean closes by norm_num.

| Parameter | Unit | Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V_DD^min, V_DD^max | V | ENV | supply range; V_DD is constant during a run |
| Temperature range | K | ENV | range over which every MEAS bound holds |
| V_OL, V_IL, V_IH, V_OH | V | MEAS/design | logic thresholds |
| V_on, V_off (per polarity) | V | MEAS | on and off gate-drive thresholds |
| I_off, I_sat, I_max (per polarity) | A | MEAS | leakage, drive and maximum current |
| G_on (per polarity) | S | MEAS | on-conductance lower bound |
| L_I (per polarity) | A/V | MODEL | Lipschitz constant of the device functions |
| C_k^min, C_k^max (per node) | F | MEAS | capacitance range, coupling at Miller factor 0 and 2, from extraction |
| T_clk, s_max | s | ENV | minimum edge spacing and skew window |
| t_low, t_high, t_slew | s | ENV | clock low and high phases, edge slew |
| t_su, t_h, t_cq^min, t_cq^max | s | MODEL (v0) | flip-flop timing, part of H_cells |
| t_pd, t_cd (per cell) | s | THM (INV); MODEL (NAND2, NOR2 in v0) | cell delays |
| t_in | s | ENV | primary-input valid time after an edge |
| η | V | MODEL | per-cycle fidelity bound |
| ε_init, ε_mfg | probability | MEAS | loading and contract failure rates |
| ε_cycle | probability | MODEL | per-cycle fidelity failure rate |
| N | cycles | spec | length of the verified run |

Consistency conditions:

- K0: 0 < V_OL < V_IL < V_IH < V_OH < V_DD^min.
- K1: V_IL ≤ V_off^n, so a valid-low input turns n-FETs off.
- K2: V_DD^min − V_IL ≥ V_on^p, so a valid-low input turns p-FETs on.
- K3: V_IH ≥ V_on^n, so a valid-high input turns n-FETs on.
- K4: V_DD^max − V_IH ≤ V_off^p, so a valid-high input turns p-FETs off.
- K5: I_off^n < min(G_on^p·(V_DD^min − V_OH), I_sat^p), so the inverter's pull-up beats leakage.
- K6: I_off^p < min(G_on^n·V_OL, I_sat^n), so the inverter's pull-down beats leakage.
- K7: η < min(NM_L, NM_H).
- K8: t_cq^max + t_su + s_max < T_clk (strict), plus the per-path setup and hold constraints of L4.
- K9: V_off < V_on for each polarity, so no gate voltage makes a device both on and off.
- K10 (stacks, for the v1 NAND2 and NOR2 proofs): V_IH − V_OL ≥ V_on^n and V_OH − V_IL ≥ V_on^p, so the upper device of a stack stays on.
- K11 (stacks, candidate): 2·I_off^p < min(G_on^n·V_OL/2, I_sat^n) for NAND2 output low, and 2·I_off^n < min(G_on^p·(V_DD^min − V_OH)/2, I_sat^p) for NOR2 output high. The v1 stack lemma confirms or replaces it.
- K12: t_low + t_high + 2·t_slew + s_max ≤ T_clk, so ClockOK waveforms exist.

### Known gaps and v1 roadmap

v0 trades physical completeness for provability. Each row below moves an assumption out of MF_k, MODEL or ENV and into THM.

| Gap in v0 | Consequence | v1 move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crosstalk outside Miller factors 0 to 2 | Same-direction speed-ups and opposite-direction glitches sit in MF_k | Explicit coupling capacitance, rails widened by the coupling ratio, robust cell theorems |
| No inductance | Ground bounce and IR drop sit in MF_k | Inductor states with a positive-definite inductance matrix |
| Clock pins are ideal ENV waveforms | Clock-tree skew and slew are assumed | A clock tree of cells, with a theorem bounding edge times and slews |
| H_cells assumed for flip-flops, NAND2 and NOR2 (MODEL) | Hold sets and stack delays are not constructed | Prove FF1–FF2 from a master–slave netlist via invariant sets (Greenstreet-style reachability, Brockett-annulus specs); series-stack lemma with K10–K11 |
| ε_cycle bounds an unobservable event | Its value rests on modeling | Bound an observable surrogate event that implies MF_k |
| Synchronous inputs only | No asynchronous I/O | Synchronizer theorem with an explicit failure rate; THM L3.3 rules out a deterministic one |
| Flip-flop memory, single-cycle core | No SRAM, caches, pipeline, interrupts or CSRs | SRAM cell contracts; pipeline refinement by flushing (Burch–Dill) |
| Initial state from loading statistics (ε_init) | Reset is not modeled | Prove reset establishes Inv_L4(enc s₀) |
| L0 bridge not formalized | KCL, KVL and THM L0.4 are justified on paper | Formalize THM L0.1–L0.4 on Mathlib (fundamental theorem of calculus, box divergence theorem) |

### Milestones

The build goes bottom-up through the deterministic core, then closes the loop with the shell. Each milestone ends in theorems with no sorry.

1. M1, L2 core: netlist types, Tellegen, well-posedness, rail invariance, continuous dependence, slew bound.
2. M2, contracts and inverter: FETContract and NodeCap, K0–K12 as norm_num checks, THM L3.1 and THM L3.2 for INV.
3. M3, Buridan: THM L3.3 from THM L2.4 and the intermediate value theorem.
4. M4, synchronous refinement: THM L3.4 and THM L4.1, under H_cells.
5. M5, ISA and core: the RV32I-subset step function, the single-cycle core, THM L5.1, the encoder lemma and one verified sample program.
6. M6, shell: THM Core, which proves hcore, and THM End-to-end, which is pure measure theory.
7. M7: the L0 bridge and the v1 items.
